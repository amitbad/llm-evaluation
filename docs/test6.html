Phase 2 — Evaluation Techniques

Test 6: Semantic Similarity Scoring

Moving beyond word counting. Using sentence embeddings and cosine similarity to evaluate whether the bot's response means the same thing as the expected answer — even when the words are completely different.

📁 phase-2-evaluation-techniques/semantic-similarity/  |  healthcare_bot.py · test6_semantic_similarity.py · report_generator.py · regenerate_report.py

Why Semantic Similarity?

BLEU and ROUGE (Test 3) measure word overlap. They count how many words in the bot's response match the expected answer. This works when you expect exact or near-exact wording. It breaks down badly in real conversation.

Expected AnswerBot ResponseBLEU ScoreSemantic ScoreReality
That service is completely free of chargeWe do not charge any fees for that serviceVery low — different wordsHigh — same meaningBot was correct
Cancel at least 24 hours in advancePlease give 24 hours notice before cancellingPartial — some overlapHigh — same meaningBot was correct
Call 911 in a medical emergencyFor emergencies contact your insurance providerLowLow — different meaningBot was wrong
⚠️ BLEU would penalise the first two responses even though the bot answered correctly. Semantic similarity evaluates meaning, not spelling.

How It Works — The Concept

Every piece of text can be converted into a list of numbers called an embedding. Think of it as a coordinate in meaning-space. Text that means similar things gets coordinates that are close together. Text that means different things gets coordinates far apart.

The library we use — sentence-transformers — does this conversion using a pre-trained model called all-MiniLM-L6-v2. It was trained on millions of sentences specifically to place semantically similar text close together in this space.

The distance between two embeddings is measured using cosine similarity — a score between 0 and 1:

💡 Thresholds are not fixed facts — they are QA decisions. We chose 0.80 as the Pass threshold. You could raise it to 0.85 for stricter evaluation or lower it to 0.75 if the domain allows more variation. After seeing real results, threshold tuning is the next step.

What We Built

A new domain — a hospital chatbot for City General Hospital — with 22 purpose-built test cases written in QA format. This is a deliberate choice: healthcare has higher stakes than banking, clearer right/wrong answers, and richer failure modes (hallucination in healthcare is dangerous, not just embarrassing).

⚠️ Critical limitation: Semantic similarity only measures how close the bot's response is to YOUR expected answer. If your expected answer is wrong, a high score means the bot agreed with your mistake. The quality of the reference answer determines the quality of the evaluation.

Key Learnings

  • Semantic similarity solves BLEU's biggest problem. BLEU fails when correct answers use different words. Semantic similarity evaluates meaning — two sentences can share zero words and still score 0.85 if they mean the same thing.
  • Partial is the dominant verdict in open-ended Q&A. 68% of our cases were Partial. This is expected — the bot often answered correctly but with different structure, additional context, or slightly different framing than the reference. Partial does not mean wrong.
  • Score alone is not enough for high-stakes cases. TC019 scored 0.71 (Partial) but gave the correct 988 crisis line. In healthcare, that is the most important thing the bot can do. Always read the actual response for safety-critical test cases — never rely on the score alone.
  • The expected answer is the baseline — write it carefully. If your reference answer is vague, the score will be meaningless. Specific, well-written expected answers produce scores you can trust. Vague expected answers produce scores that tell you nothing.
  • Threshold tuning is a QA decision, not a data science one. Where you draw the Pass/Fail line depends on how much variation the domain tolerates. Healthcare might need a stricter threshold than a general information bot. You decide — the score is input to that decision, not the decision itself.
  • Domain choice matters for evaluation design. Healthcare introduced failure modes the banking bot never had — crisis queries, drug interaction questions, medical records privacy. Different domains surface different weaknesses. Choosing the right domain for your test cases is part of evaluation strategy.

What This Evaluation is Called

This is Semantic Similarity Evaluation using dense vector embeddings — a form of representation learning where text is converted into high-dimensional vectors and compared mathematically. The specific measure used is cosine similarity. In industry this technique also underlies retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) — where the same embedding comparison is used to find relevant documents to give to the model. Understanding embeddings here is the foundation for understanding RAG in Phase 3.
