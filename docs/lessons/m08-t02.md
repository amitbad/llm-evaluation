## What You Will Learn

- Decide whether the passages actually supplied contain enough authorized evidence for a specific question.
- Check the answer against the governing policy and trace each factual claim to its evidence.
- Diagnose missing, fabricated, stale, and misleading citations, and judge when an assistant should abstain.
- Report the failing stage, calculate rates with visible denominators, and preserve the original result when testing a repair.

## Why This Lesson Matters

A customer asks Northstar Support, “Can I get a refund after 45 days without a receipt?” Read the one passage placed in the assistant’s prompt. “Refund requests require a valid receipt and must be filed within 30 days of purchase.” Before reading the saved reply, decide what you would tell the customer.

The assistant replies, “Yes, refunds are available within 90 days.” You can point to two problems with that decision. Day 45 is outside the 30-day window, and the customer has no receipt. The saved prompt shows that the necessary evidence reached the assistant. Start by investigating how it used that evidence.

## Beginner Explanation

### Look at the passage before the answer

The shipping page from the previous chapter mentions 45 days. That overlap can move it up the search ranking. Imagine the retriever instead returned the refund help desk passage, “Refund requests are handled by the customer-support department.” It is about refunds, so it is **relevant**. Could you tell this customer whether their request qualifies? The passage gives neither the deadline nor the proof requirement.

Ask what a complete answer needs. The review sheet lists the deadline measured from purchase and the requirement for a valid receipt. Read the exact **context**, the authorized passages delivered to the assistant for this request. Mark which facts those passages establish, then assign a **context sufficiency** label.

- **Sufficient.** The context establishes both requirements. Day 45 is outside the 30-day window and the customer lacks the required receipt.
- **Partial.** The context gives the deadline alone. It can explain why day 45 misses the deadline. This exercise also requires the response to address the receipt, so the context cannot support the complete answer.
- **Insufficient.** The context only tells you which department handles refunds. Neither required fact is available.
- **Contradictory.** Two eligible current passages disagree about a required fact and the writer has no approved rule for resolving their disagreement. Record the disagreement even if one passage alone would appear complete.

Predict how the deadline-only passage would score if the question asked just “What is the deadline?” It would be sufficient. Judge against the requested task, using the actual passages shown to the assistant. A gold passage, the correct source named on the review sheet, may appear high in the search results and still be cut from the delivered context.

### Read the answer against two different sources of evidence

Consider a correct reply in your inbox. It says a refund request is due within 30 days of purchase and requires a valid receipt. Open the signed policy and you can verify both facts. Now open the saved prompt. Its only passage is about shipping delays. Nothing in the prompt can justify either refund claim.

The first check is **correctness** against the authoritative policy in effect for this customer and date. The second is **groundedness**, sometimes called **faithfulness**, against the supplied context. For an applied answer such as “Your request is outside the window,” check the inference too. Day 45 is later than the stated deadline of day 30. Finding `30` in the passage gives you a fact to use, and you still have to apply it to the customer’s question.

Compare six small replays. In the first four the correct refund passage and the unrelated shipping passage are both available. `DOC-REFUND-01` and `DOC-SHIPPING-02` are display IDs for those source documents. The full lab uses versioned passage IDs.

- **A.** “Yes, refunds are available within 90 days.” The policy contradicts the answer. No citation was supplied.
- **B.** “No. The policy requires a valid receipt and a request within 30 days.” The decision and both requirements are correct and supported. Required citations are missing.
- **C.** “No. The refund period is 30 days, and a receipt is required. [DOC-REFUND-01]” The refund passage supports the claims and the decision follows from the customer’s stated facts.
- **D.** “No. The refund period is 30 days, and a receipt is required. [DOC-SHIPPING-02]” The refund passage still supports the claims, but the cited shipping passage does not.
- **C with shipping context alone.** The same reply stays correct against approved policy. The retrieved context lacks support, and the cited refund passage was never supplied.
- **Warranty reply backed by a stale guide.** “Email Support to start a warranty claim.” The old policy supports this wording. The current order-page rule makes the answer wrong and the citation stale.

In B through D, the term “refund period” is understood from the purchase date in the question and supplied rule. In a standalone answer, prefer “within 30 days of purchase.” Answer C gives the reader a way to verify both facts. D can fail the citation check even when its wording is identical and authorized support exists elsewhere.

Use **correct** when the answer establishes every required fact and makes no wrong or unverifiable factual additions. A **partially correct** answer gets some required facts right while omitting or mishandling others. An **incorrect** answer gives a wrong policy value and establishes none of the required facts. An **unverifiable** claim concerns something the available governing record cannot establish. If the reply makes only claims you cannot verify, label it unverifiable. If it gives verified but irrelevant facts and omits everything asked for, label it incomplete. Separately record any critical violation, including 90 days in place of 30. The lab marks that error as blocking regardless of anything else the response gets right.

### Check a multi-claim reply one statement at a time

Read “Refunds are available within 30 days of purchase, require a valid receipt, and are processed within two hours.” Underline the deadline, the receipt requirement, and the processing time. Each can be checked on its own. This is an **atomic claim**, a statement small enough for one evidence judgment. Keep conditions, units, and starting points in the claim. “30 days” without “of purchase” could match the wrong rule.

For each claim, copy the relevant wording and note its passage ID and version. A claim can be **supported**, **unsupported**, or **unknown** if part of the investigation record is missing. Where available, record whether an unsupported claim contradicts the text or adds something absent from it. For the reply above, the approved evidence supports two claims out of three. The two-hour promise may be an invented detail, often called a **hallucination**. The evidence establishes that the reply lacked support for it. You would need a governing processing-time source to decide whether the promise is false.

### Follow each citation to the exact claim

Read a citation as a pointer. First ask whether the answer gave a reference. Then ask whether the ID resolves to a known source, whether the source is authorized and was supplied, and whether its version is approved for this question. Now read the cited wording. If it establishes the claim, the link provides **entailment**, meaning you can justify the claim from that wording and any explicit case facts. A shipping passage may be a real, accessible source and still fail this check for a refund deadline.

A document-level link names a whole policy, a passage-level link names an exact span such as `RF-v2:s01`, and a claim-level link attaches the evidence to the specific statement it supports. The last two can be used together. A document link can suffice in a short unambiguous policy. In a long policy with changing rules, a span and version make the result reproducible.

Suppose `RF-v2:s01` gives only the deadline and `RF-v2:s02` gives only the receipt requirement in a teaching variant. Cite the first for the deadline and the second for the receipt. If one claim needs both, link both and record how the two passages together support it. Two citations attached to a paragraph do not establish which statement either supports. **Citation completeness**, or coverage in this lab, is the share of claims with valid supporting citations. **Citation precision**, reported as citation validity rate here, is the share of supplied claim–reference links that pass all the link checks. Repeated copies of a link get one vote and a duplicate diagnostic.

### Resolve source authority and decide when to stop

A current warranty policy says to start from the order page. An archived copy says to email Support. Northstar’s signed policy register identifies the governing clause for the policy snapshot and who may read it. A newly uploaded FAQ can be older in substance or lower in authority than the approved policy. Inspect the effective period and precedence rule for the specific question. If there is a clear winner for the reviewer but the assistant received two competing active passages with no resolution instructions, record the delivery conflict and make the precedence rule visible to the assistant. If the evidence cannot be resolved for either party, route to review.

A staff-only note can contain helpful wording. It remains outside the public evidence set. A trace that supplied or cited it to a public caller flags a critical authorization failure. Record the alert without reproducing the restricted passage in a customer-facing report.

If the question asks whether a particular parcel arrived and the supplied context has only a general shipping policy, “I can’t verify this parcel’s status from the information I have” is **abstention**, an explicit choice to withhold an unsupported answer. It is appropriate here. Giving the same response when the user asked for a deadline and the approved deadline was supplied would be unnecessary. An empty reply is a missing answer to investigate. It gives no evidence of deliberate abstention. Track how many answers and abstentions you could score so that a high score on a small answered subset cannot hide routine refusal.

### Turn the observations into a diagnosis

- **Required evidence exists but was missing from the prompt.** Check the retrieval ranking and the later context selection. If the case genuinely has no authorized evidence in the corpus, test abstention rather than treating the gap as a search defect.
- **Sufficient evidence was present and the answer misapplied it.** Inspect how the writer used the rule. Preserve the exact question, delivered context, and reply.
- **The answer is correct and the reference points to the wrong passage.** Check how citations are attached to claims.
- **Eligible passages disagree.** Investigate context resolution. Record stale passages independently and compare them with the approved version.
- **Restricted text was supplied or cited.** Record an authorization failure even if the public policy happens to agree with the reply. Check access controls before tuning relevance.

A single result can have several causes or symptoms. Annotate the evidence for each, and avoid inferring the system’s hidden reasoning from a final answer. In a controlled replay, keep the question, authoritative policy snapshot, rubric, and all unrelated inputs fixed. A repair of a saved annotation or citation proves the checker responds to that edit. You need new captured responses to measure a change to the generator.

### Count with the right denominators

Suppose you reviewed four cases and three had sufficient context. Two cases had annotated factual answers and one was fully correct. The other two explicitly abstained and one refusal was appropriate. Suppose those answers made four claims, three supported by the context, and only one answer had every claim supported. They supplied three distinct claim–reference links. Two were valid and each supported a different claim.

- Context sufficiency is `3/4` known context judgments.
- Answer correctness is `1/2` annotated factual answers. Grounded-answer rate is `1/2` annotated answers with scorable context.
- Claim support is `3/4` scorable claims, with one unsupported claim.
- Citation validity is `2/3` supplied links. Citation coverage is `2/4` claims with at least one valid link.
- Appropriate abstention is `1/2` judged explicit abstentions. Report the actual number of abstentions as well.
- If a case promised 90 days, report one critical refund-policy failure. Count affected cases, even if multiple wrong refund claims appear in one reply.

Notice how an unanswered case and an unsupported claim enter different denominators. In the lab, missing reply text and missing annotations are reported for review and excluded from answer metrics. A missing context record is unknown for sufficiency and support. When zero items qualify for a measure, print N/A and the zero denominator. Do not treat missing evidence as a passing score. The fictional decision policy gives **PASS** for a complete, correct, supported, cited answer with adequate context, or for a justified abstention on a reviewed unanswerable case. **FAIL** blocks confirmed critical refund or authorization failures and unsupported factual claims. **REVIEW** is for unresolved contradictions, incomplete records, noncritical defects, and cases that need another look. One blocking case makes the lab suite FAIL.

## Worked Demonstration

### Example 1. Is the context sufficient?

**User question.** “Can I get a refund after 45 days without a receipt?”

**Approved reference.** “Refund requests require a valid receipt and must be filed within 30 days of purchase.” The evaluation requires the deadline and receipt rule.

Consider three alternative contexts before reading the verdicts.

1. `RF-v2:s01` contains the complete policy. Point to “within 30 days of purchase” and “valid receipt.” Both are available, so label **sufficient**.
2. A teaching variant `RF-TIME-v1:s01` contains only “Refund requests must be filed within 30 days of purchase.” The learner can evaluate the time limit but lacks evidence for the proof requirement. Label **partial**.
3. `REF-HELP-v1:s01` reads “Refund requests are handled by the customer-support department.” It confirms the topic but neither fact. Label **insufficient**.

**Actual LLM response.** In the third fictional replay, the assistant says, “I can’t verify the conditions from this information.”

**Assessment.** With that context the assistant should abstain. It makes no factual policy claim, so there are no answer claims or citations to score. The approved policy is known to exist and did not reach the writer.

**Failure classification.** Missing required context, with appropriate abstention. Inspect the top-k results and prompt assembly before editing the answer prompt.

### Example 2. Is the answer supported?

**User question.** “How long do I have to request a refund, what proof is required, and how fast will it be processed?”

**Approved reference.** `RF-v2:s01` establishes the purchase-based deadline and valid receipt rule. The approved record supplied for review gives no processing-time promise.

**Actual LLM response.** “Refunds are available within 30 days of purchase, require a valid receipt, and are processed within two hours.” The fictional capture shows `RF-v2:s01` as its only context.

1. “Within 30 days of purchase” → `RF-v2:s01`, “within 30 days of purchase.” Supported and correct.
2. “Require a valid receipt” → `RF-v2:s01`, “require a valid receipt.” Supported and correct.
3. “Processed within two hours” → no supporting span. Unsupported and unverifiable against the approved record.

**Assessment.** Put a check beside the deadline and another beside the receipt requirement. Leave the processing-time row unconfirmed. That gives `2 supported claims / 3 factual claims`. The context is partial for the three-part question. If Northstar has no approved processing-time source, the response can explain the two documented requirements and say it cannot confirm processing time. The saved two-hour promise fails the unsupported-claim gate. Compare a second fictional reply from the warranty case: “Email Support to start a warranty claim.” The supplied `WA-v1:s01` says exactly that, so the claim is supported, but the signed register now points to `WA-v2:s01`, which directs customers to their order page. That reply is wrong against current policy.

**Failure classification.** In the refund example, inspect the unsupported addition and request a governing processing-time source or abstention on that part. In the warranty example, investigate stale context and citation as well as the wrong policy result. A high groundedness score would hide the issue.

### Example 3. Do the citations support the claims?

**User question.** “What is the refund deadline and what proof must I keep?”

**Approved reference.** `RF-v2:s01` gives 30 days from purchase with a valid receipt. The delivered `SH-v1:s01` gives a 45-day shipping-contact threshold.

**Actual LLM response.** Three fictional captures used the same context: “You have 30 days from purchase with a valid receipt [RF-v2:s01].” “You have 30 days from purchase with a valid receipt [SH-v1:s01].” “You have 90 days from purchase [RF-v2:s01].”

1. Capture one’s reference resolves to the current supplied policy. Mark both deadline and receipt claims supported and link each to that passage. There are two valid claim–reference links.
2. Capture two’s reference also resolves. Read the text. Shipping supports neither refund claim. The reply remains correct and grounded by the refund passage, but valid citation coverage is `0/2`.
3. Capture three’s reference exists and is current, but it says 30, not 90. The answer is wrong and unsupported. The citation is invalid for that claim, and the critical refund-policy gate fails.

**Assessment.** Changing just the second capture’s citation to `RF-v2:s01` changes citation validity and coverage without changing the answer claims. A fabricated ID would fail earlier, at resolution.

**Failure classification.** Capture two has a citation attachment problem. Capture three has a policy and answer-use failure as well as a citation error. Repair each failure separately and rerun the checks.

### Step 1 — Check whether the context is sufficient

Place the two requirements for the refund question in a set. A Python set holds each item once. A dictionary holds named values, which you read with square brackets. The expression `required - found` keeps the items still missing, `&` finds items in both sets, and `sorted` gives predictable output. `if` and `elif` evaluate the cases in order.

question = {

retrieved = {"deadline", "receipt"}
required = question["required"]
missing = required - retrieved

if not missing:
    label = "SUFFICIENT"
elif required & retrieved:
    label = "PARTIAL"
else:
    label = "INSUFFICIENT"

print(question["id"], label)
print("Missing:", ", ".join(sorted(missing)) or "none")

### Step 2 — Check the answer against policy

Check the deadline and receipt statements independently against the approved values. `.items()` gives each field and its value, `.get()` returns the value stored for a key, and `==` compares values. A dictionary comprehension makes one verdict for each required fact. `sum` adds 1 for a true check and 0 for a false one.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
policy = {"deadline": 30, "receipt": "required"}
claims = {"deadline": 30, "receipt": "required"}
matches = {key: claims.get(key) == value for key, value in policy.items()}
for key, matches_policy in matches.items():
    print(key, "matches" if matches_policy else "wrong or missing")
count = sum(matches.values())
if count == len(policy) and all(key in policy for key in claims):
    verdict = "CORRECT"
elif count:
    verdict = "PARTIALLY CORRECT"
else:
    verdict = "INCORRECT"
print(verdict)
```

CORRECT

### Step 7 — Report a citation repair without erasing the failure

Evaluate one complete case with a two-claim answer and an unrelated citation. A set comprehension lists the fields in the retrieved evidence. `frozenset` is a set that cannot be edited, and a tuple holds the original citation sequence. The repair creates a new tuple. The comparison table shows sufficiency, correctness, support and decision side by side. Each audit row records the case, supplied IDs, answer, citation decisions, and gate result.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
query = "How long do I have and what proof do I need?"
policy = {"deadline": 30, "receipt": "required"}
claims = (("deadline", 30), ("receipt", "required"))
sources = {
    "RF-v2:s01": frozenset(claims),
    "SH-v1:s01": frozenset({("shipping_delay", 45)}),
}
retrieved = ("RF-v2:s01", "SH-v1:s01")
evidence = frozenset().union(*(sources[key] for key in retrieved))
original = ("RF-v2:s01", "SH-v1:s01")
repair = False

def evaluate(run, refs):
    sufficient = policy.keys() <= {key for key, value in evidence}
    correct = dict(claims) == policy
    supported = sum(claim in evidence for claim in claims)
    labels = tuple("VALID" if claim in sources.get(ref, ()) and ref in retrieved
                   else "INVALID" for claim, ref in zip(claims, refs))
    critical = any(key == "deadline" and value != policy[key] for key, value in claims)
    if critical or supported < len(claims):
        decision = "FAIL"
    elif sufficient and correct and all(label == "VALID" for label in labels):
        decision = "PASS"
    else:
        decision = "REVIEW"
    return (run, refs, sufficient, correct, supported, labels, critical, decision)

history = (evaluate("original", original),)
if repair:
    history += (evaluate("repair", (original[0], "RF-v2:s01")),)

print("Q1:", query)
print("Retrieved:", ", ".join(retrieved))
print("Claims:", claims)
print("Run | Context sufficient | Correct | Claim support | Decision")
for run, refs, sufficient, correct, supported, labels, critical, decision in history:
    print(f"{run} | {sufficient} | {correct} | {supported}/2 | {decision}")
print("Citation audit:")
for run, refs, sufficient, correct, supported, labels, critical, decision in history:
    print(f"{run}: {refs} -> {labels}")
    print(f"  sufficient={sufficient} correct={correct} support={supported}/2")
    print(f"  abstention=N/A critical={critical} decision={decision}")
```

    for case in records:
        if not case.id or case.id in ids or not case.question or not case.required:
            raise ValueError("Expected a unique case ID, a question and required facts")
        if case.behavior not in {"ANSWER", "ABSTAIN"} or (case.behavior == "ABSTAIN" and case.claims):

def evaluate(case, run):
    visible = tuple(ref for ref in case.delivered if can_read(ref))
    unknown_context = any(ref not in passages for ref in case.delivered)
    critical = set()
    reasons = set()
    if any(ref in passages and not can_read(ref) for ref in case.delivered):
        critical.add("authorization")
    available = {fact for ref in visible for fact in passages[ref].facts}
    approved = {fact for ref in visible if current(passages[ref]) for fact in passages[ref].facts}
    needed = set(case.required)
    present = {key for key, value in approved if key in policy and policy[key] == value}
    conflict = any(key in needed and value != policy.get(key) for key, value in approved)
    if unknown_context:
        context = "UNKNOWN"
        reasons.add("missing passage record")
    elif conflict:
        context = "CONTRADICTORY"
        reasons.add("context conflict")
    elif needed <= present:
        context = "SUFFICIENT"
    elif needed & present:
        context = "PARTIAL"
    else:
        context = "INSUFFICIENT"
    if context in {"PARTIAL", "INSUFFICIENT"} and needed <= policy.keys():
        reasons.add("required evidence missing")
    if any(not current(passages[ref]) for ref in visible):
        reasons.add("stale context")

    abstention = "N/A"
    correctness = "N/A"
    rows = []
    if not case.reply.strip():
        reasons.add("empty answer")
    elif case.behavior == "ABSTAIN":
        abstention = ("UNNECESSARY" if context == "SUFFICIENT" else
                      "UNKNOWN" if context == "UNKNOWN" else "APPROPRIATE")

            reasons.add("unnecessary abstention")
    elif not case.claims:
        reasons.add("missing claim annotations")

        mismatches = 0
        for claim in case.claims:
            key, value = claim.key, claim.value
            fact = (key, value)
            if key in policy and policy[key] == value:
                matched.add(key)
                truth = "MATCH"
            elif key in policy:
                mismatches += 1
                truth = "WRONG"
                reasons.add("wrong policy value")
                if key in {"refund_days", "receipt"}:
                    critical.add("refund policy")
            else:
                truth = "UNVERIFIABLE"
                reasons.add("claim outside policy key")
            support = ("UNKNOWN" if unknown_context else
                       "SUPPORTED" if fact in available else "UNSUPPORTED")
            if support == "UNSUPPORTED":
                reasons.add("unsupported claim")
            citations = []
            for ref in dict.fromkeys(claim.refs):
                if ref not in passages:
                    label = "UNKNOWN ID"
                elif not can_read(ref):
                    label = "RESTRICTED"
                    critical.add("authorization")
                elif ref not in visible:
                    label = "NOT SUPPLIED"
                elif not current(passages[ref]):
                    label = "STALE"
                elif fact not in passages[ref].facts:
                    label = "DOES NOT SUPPORT"
                else:
                    label = "VALID"
                citations.append(("[restricted]" if label == "RESTRICTED" else ref, label))
                if label != "VALID":
                    reasons.add("citation error")
            if not citations:
                reasons.add("missing citation")
            if len(claim.refs) != len(citations):
                reasons.add("duplicate citation")
            rows.append((key, truth, support, tuple(citations)))

        elif matched & needed:
            correctness = "PARTIALLY CORRECT"
        elif mismatches:
            correctness = "INCORRECT"
        elif all(truth == "UNVERIFIABLE" for _, truth, _, _ in rows):
            correctness = "UNVERIFIABLE"
        else:
            correctness = "INCOMPLETE"
            reasons.add("required facts omitted")

    if critical or "unsupported claim" in reasons:
        decision = "FAIL"
    elif context == "SUFFICIENT" and correctness == "CORRECT" and not reasons:
        decision = "PASS"
    elif (abstention == "APPROPRIATE" and context == "INSUFFICIENT"

        decision = "PASS"
    else:
        decision = "REVIEW"
    return Audit(case, run, snapshot, rubric, visible, context, correctness, tuple(rows),

    print(f"{row.case.id}: {row.context} | {row.correctness} | abstention={row.abstention} | {row.decision}")
    print(f"  supplied public: {ids}")
    for key, truth, support, refs in row.claims:
        links = ", ".join(f"{ref}={label}" for ref, label in refs) or "MISSING"
        print(f"  {key}: {truth}, {support}, {links}")
    reasons = ", ".join(row.reasons) or "none"
    critical = ", ".join(row.critical) or "none"
    print(f"  diagnostic: {reasons} | critical: {critical}")

def fraction(count, total):
    return f"{count}/{total}" if total else "N/A (0 eligible)"

def summarize(rows):
    known = [row for row in rows if row.context != "UNKNOWN"]
    answered = [row for row in rows if row.claims]
    claims = [claim for row in answered for claim in row.claims]
    scorable = [claim for claim in claims if claim[2] != "UNKNOWN"]
    links = [label for _, _, _, cites in claims for _, label in cites]
    abstained = [row for row in rows if row.abstention in {"APPROPRIATE", "UNNECESSARY"}]
    unsupported = sum(claim[2] == "UNSUPPORTED" for claim in scorable)
    rates = {
        "context": fraction(sum(row.context == "SUFFICIENT" for row in known), len(known)),
        "correct": fraction(sum(row.correctness == "CORRECT" for row in answered), len(answered)),
        "grounded": fraction(sum(all(c[2] == "SUPPORTED" for c in row.claims)
                                 for row in answered if row.context != "UNKNOWN"),
                             sum(row.context != "UNKNOWN" for row in answered)),
        "support": fraction(len(scorable) - unsupported, len(scorable)),
        "citations": fraction(links.count("VALID"), len(links)),
        "citation coverage": fraction(sum(any(label == "VALID" for _, label in c[3])
                                          for c in claims), len(claims)),
        "abstention": fraction(sum(row.abstention == "APPROPRIATE" for row in abstained), len(abstained)),
    }
    print("Rates:", " | ".join(f"{key}={value}" for key, value in rates.items()))
    print(f"Coverage: cases={len(rows)} context scored={len(known)} answers scored={len(answered)}"
          f" support claims scored={len(scorable)}")
    print(f"Counts: unsupported={unsupported} refund policy failures="
          f"{sum('refund policy' in row.critical for row in rows)}"
          f" authorization failures={sum('authorization' in row.critical for row in rows)}")
    decision = ("FAIL" if any(row.decision == "FAIL" for row in rows) else
                "PASS" if rows and all(row.decision == "PASS" for row in rows) else "REVIEW")
    print("Suite decision:", decision)

## Common Mistakes

- Scoring the top-ranked passage instead of checking what entered the prompt.
- Giving a claim credit because its number occurs elsewhere in the document.
- Counting a link that merely opens as evidence for the attached statement.
- Treating a blank reply as successful abstention, or scoring unknown evidence as zero.
- Replacing an old audit entry when a label or citation changes.
