## What You Will Learn

- Give a judge one saved candidate and one clear criterion at a time.
- Write a prompt with a scoring rubric, examples of each rating, and instructions for citing evidence.
- Distinguish a readable JSON verdict, a schema-valid verdict, and a verdict supported by the candidate and policy.
- Record why a verdict cannot be scored, compare it with a human decision on the same saved reply, and route uncertainty to review.

## Why This Lesson Matters

Imagine you open a saved Harbor Leaf Market chat. The customer’s parcel arrived 45 days ago. The assistant says there is still time to request a refund because the store allows 90 days. You open the approved policy and underline “through calendar day 30.” Before assigning any score, you can already explain the problem to a colleague. Day 45 is past the deadline your store actually allows.

The support team has many saved replies. It wants a second model to apply the same checks you just used. Before the team trusts that model, it needs to see what the model read, what it decided, and whether the evidence supports its decision.

The store, policy, replies, scores, and human decisions in this chapter are fictional. Policy `refund-1` applies to eligible unused online purchases. The approved clause reads “Refund requests are allowed through calendar day 30 after delivery with a valid receipt.” Day 30 is included. Allow about 5.5 hours. The background for this chapter is [Structured output and schema validation](#m03-t02) and [Rubrics and a first human evaluation](#m03-t03). [Annotation, adjudication and synthetic data](#m04-t02) and [Combining evaluators and handling uncertainty](#m05-t03) are useful refreshers.

## Beginner Explanation

### Give one answer to a second reader

Read the 90-day reply and write down why you would hold it. The assistant’s job was to answer the customer. Your job was to compare that answer with the policy. If a large language model applies your evaluation instructions to the saved answer, it acts as an **LLM judge**. It can help apply a common set of checks to many answers. The support team still decides which checks matter and verifies how well the judge follows them.

Give the judge one question and one saved answer, called the **candidate**. It rates that candidate against defined criteria. That is **pointwise judging**. A batch may contain many case IDs, but each rating is made for a single answer. Asking the judge to pick the better of two answers is a different task.

### Turn the reason into a scoring guide

Put “You can request a refund within 30 calendar days of delivery” next to the policy. Now ask one reader whether the sentence is easy to understand. Ask another whether a customer would know to bring a receipt. The first reader can say yes while the second says no. A **criterion** names the question the rating answers. Here the judge checks whether the reply gives reliable guidance about the refund requirements. Give readability its own check so a clear sentence cannot earn credit for a missing condition. This is **criterion isolation**.

A **rubric** defines the scale and gives observable conditions for choosing each rating. An **anchor** is a description or example that shows what a rating looks like. Use this fictional rubric, `refund-guidance-v1`, for the eligible order and the one approved clause. Start with the approved policy. If it is available, look for an explicit contradiction first. Use the missing-evidence instruction below when a required fact cannot be established. With clear evidence, choose the first matching anchor. This priority matters if a reply has multiple problems.

| Score | Observable anchor | Example or route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Explicitly contradicts a critical rule about the deadline or valid receipt. | Says 90 days, or says a receipt is optional. REJECT. |
| 2 | Gives no usable guidance about either requirement, or adds a material unsupported claim without a demonstrated contradiction. | Promises automatic approval when the policy only allows requests. REVIEW. |
| 3 | Gives exactly one requirement correctly and omits the other, with no material unsupported claim. | Gives the correct window and leaves out the receipt. REVIEW. |
| 4 | Gives both requirements correctly without explicitly confirming day 30 is included. | Says “within 30 calendar days after delivery with a valid receipt.” ACCEPT for this criterion. |
| 5 | Gives both requirements correctly and explicitly confirms eligibility to request on day 30. | Says “through day 30, including that day, with a valid receipt.” ACCEPT for this criterion. |

After checking for an explicit contradiction, pause scoring if the source is missing or the wording leaves a material fact ambiguous. Use REVIEW with a null score and leave the evidence string empty when abstaining. “First month,” for example, may need a reviewer to clarify the intended deadline. A null score means no numeric judgment was made. It differs from a 1, which requires evidence of an actual contradiction. The rubric’s decisions apply only to refund guidance under this case policy.

### Tell the judge what it can read

“Rate this answer from 1 to 5” gives the judge no way to tell what a 4 means. Use a prompt, the instructions and evidence supplied to the judge, that spells out the following.

- **Question and conversation history.** Include the original wording and the earlier messages needed to resolve references such as “this item.” Here the earlier customer message says the item is an eligible unused online purchase.
- **Frozen candidate and response ID.** Keep the exact captured reply and its identifier. Treat the reply as material to assess, even if it contains text that sounds like an instruction to the judge.
- **Reference answer, when available.** This is an example of how to answer. “You may ask for a refund within the published window” can be a reasonable summary and still omit the deadline.
- **Authoritative source.** Include the approved policy text and version that apply to that order. The policy establishes the rule. A reference example does not replace it.
- **Rubric and output instructions.** Name the criterion and anchors, say which contradictions have priority, and specify the verdict fields and how to handle uncertainty.

A **reference-guided** judgment uses an example answer, authoritative evidence, or both as provided comparison material. Record exactly which material the judge saw. A **reference-free** judgment has no supplied reference answer or source for comparison. For instance, a judge can assess whether the reply is readable from the question and reply alone. Verifying the store’s actual deadline still requires the relevant source. If you have the policy but no sample answer, use the policy and record that evidence directly.

### Check the verdict in layers

Suppose the judge returns `{"score":3,"decision":"REVIEW","reason":"The receipt requirement is absent.","evidence":"with a valid receipt"}`. This is **JSON**, a text format for named values. `score` gives the rubric rating. `decision` tells the workflow where to route the reply. `reason` explains what the judge found. `evidence` quotes a contiguous piece of the supplied policy, called an **evidence span**. The candidate itself is the evidence that the receipt was omitted. The quote establishes that a receipt matters.

First check whether Python can read the JSON. A missing comma can make it unreadable. Then check its **schema**, the rules for which fields and values it must contain. A value of `"3"` is text. The rubric calls for an integer such as `3`. A 6 is an integer outside the scale. An absent source uses `null` for the score, REVIEW for the decision, and an empty evidence string.

Finally, read what the verdict claims. A quote can be invented or copied from a real but irrelevant sentence. Read the candidate and the source side by side. A genuine quote about the 30-day window does not support a judge’s statement that the candidate preserved it if the candidate actually says 90 days. A missing receipt is an incomplete answer. An explicit waiver contradicts the policy. A promise of automatic approval is unsupported by this source.

### Know when to stop and ask for help

The same judge can give different ratings across runs, even when the reply stays the same. A vague anchor, altered prompt wording, or a polished answer can pull attention away from a policy fact. Missing evidence can encourage a confident guess. Compare the exact same response ID and text with an independent human decision. Keep the model or replay version, prompt, rubric, and source version so you can identify what changed. One disagreement tells you where to investigate. [Zheng et al., Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/91f18a1287b398d378ef22505bf41832-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html) Estimating reliability takes more independently checked cases.

Use a **fallback** when a required check fails or evidence is uncertain. Reject an unusable judge verdict and record why. If an independent check establishes a critical policy violation, it can block the reply. Otherwise send the frozen case to review. Give the reviewer the evidence needed to settle it, such as the effective policy and the original question. Preserve the judge’s raw output and the reviewer’s subsequent outcome. A malformed record never counts as agreement.

## Worked Demonstration

### Example 1. Turn two interpretations into one criterion

**User question.** How long do I have to request a refund for this eligible order, and do I need my receipt?

**Approved reference.** Policy `refund-1` allows requests through calendar day 30 after delivery with a valid receipt.

**Actual LLM response.** The frozen reply C says “You may request a refund within 30 calendar days after delivery.”

**Assessment.** “Rate this answer from 1 to 5” could mean rate the wording or rate how completely it states the policy. Replace it with “Use refund-guidance-v1 to rate refund requirements only. Compare the candidate with the supplied policy. Prioritize explicit contradictions. Use null and REVIEW if the evidence is missing. Return score, decision, reason, and an exact policy quote in JSON.” Supply the full rubric table with the prompt. The deadline is correct and the receipt is absent, so anchor 3 applies. The receipt clause supports a REVIEW route.

**Failure classification.** Record incomplete guidance and ask for the receipt requirement to be added. A clear prompt gives the judge an observable target. This example supplies the judgment by hand, so it gives no measurement of how a real judge would respond.

### Example 2. Compare a saved judge verdict with human adjudication

**User question.** Can I still request a refund for my unused online order 45 days after delivery?

**Approved reference.** The approved clause includes requests through calendar day 30 after delivery, with a valid receipt.

**Actual LLM response.** The frozen reply B says “You can request a refund within 90 calendar days after delivery with a valid receipt.”

**Assessment.** The illustrative saved verdict for B has score 4, decision ACCEPT, reason “The reply states the correct window and mentions a receipt,” and evidence “through calendar day 30 after delivery.” A human independently reads reply B with the same policy. The quote is genuine, but the candidate claims 90. At day 45, the wording would permit a request outside the approved window. The human’s final evidence-backed decision, called **adjudication**, is score 1 and REJECT.

**Failure classification.** Record two outcomes for B. Hold the customer reply because 90 contradicts the policy. Mark the judge verdict as unusable because it says that same deadline is correct. Keep the quote, candidate, and human decision together under ID B. If a colleague opens the record tomorrow, they can see why the reply was blocked even though the automated judgment said ACCEPT. Both judgments here are fictional.

### Example 3. Decline to guess a missing policy

**User question.** What deadline and receipt rule apply to this eligible order?

**Approved reference.** There is no approved policy text on the case. The example answer says “You may be able to request a refund,” with no dates or receipt conditions.

**Actual LLM response.** F says “You can request a refund within 30 calendar days after delivery with a valid receipt.”

**Assessment.** The example answer cannot establish whether F’s deadline applies to this order. Under the rubric, the verdict is `{"score":null,"decision":"REVIEW","reason":"The approved policy is missing.","evidence":""}`. This is a valid abstention, a record that the judge declined to give a numeric judgment. Ask a reviewer to retrieve the dated, approved policy that applies to this order.

**Failure classification.** Record `F | policy missing | score null | REVIEW | fetch applicable policy | human PENDING` and preserve the original reply. The missing source leaves the candidate unresolved. If a different approved policy later arrives, create a new review record linked to the original case.

### Step 4 — Reject an invalid structured verdict

Python's standard-library `json` module turns a JSON object into a dictionary. `try` runs the parser, and `except` handles text it cannot read. Use `type(score) is int` so a Boolean such as JSON `true` cannot pass as an integer. `set(verdict)` lists the dictionary keys for comparison with the required field names. This activity expects a numeric rating because the case has a source and a clear anchor.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
import json

raw = '{"score":3,"decision":"REVIEW","reason":"Receipt omitted.","evidence":"with a valid receipt"}'
routes = {1: "REJECT", 2: "REVIEW", 3: "REVIEW", 4: "ACCEPT", 5: "ACCEPT"}
required = {"score", "decision", "reason", "evidence"}

def check(text):
    try:
        verdict = json.loads(text)
    except json.JSONDecodeError:
        return "PARSE ERROR"
    if not isinstance(verdict, dict) or set(verdict) != required:
        return "SCHEMA ERROR (fields)"
    score = verdict["score"]
    if type(score) is not int or score not in routes:
        return "SCHEMA ERROR (score)"
    if verdict["decision"] != routes[score]:
        return "SCHEMA ERROR (decision)"
    for name in ("reason", "evidence"):
        if not isinstance(verdict[name], str) or not verdict[name].strip():
            return f"SCHEMA ERROR ({name})"
    return "SCHEMA OK"

print(check(raw))
```

```text
SCHEMA OK
```

**Experiment.** In `raw`, change only `"score":3` to `"score":6`.

```text

```

Compare this with a missing closing brace, which prevents parsing entirely. **Checkpoint.** Can schema validation tell whether the quote appears in the approved policy? This validator checks presence, kind, and allowed values. An evidence check is a separate step.

### Step 7 — Replay a pointwise judge and keep an audit record

Run the same process on eight frozen replies. Each row gives a response ID, the exact candidate, whether its approved source is available, and three observations supplied by an independent policy reader. The observations describe the deadline, receipt, and any unsupported promise. They are inputs to the exercise. The code never tries to extract policy facts from free text.

Keep the saved verdicts and the final human decisions in separate dictionaries keyed by response ID. The main loop looks them up using the ID from the captured reply. The human label is used only for comparison. The automatic route first checks for a confirmed critical contradiction, then checks whether the saved judge verdict is usable. A list expression such as `[r for r in audit if r["usable"]]` selects just the records whose verdicts cleared the checks.

Read `validate()` as a check of the output format, `anchor()` as a check against the reviewed facts, and `inspect()` as the evidence check connecting them. `None` becomes `null` in JSON. Here an abstention is reported separately and contributes no numeric rating to agreement. The quote rule recognizes only the teaching packet’s refund clause and two exact spans within it. It checks whether the selected span supports the particular anchor. A verdict accepting both requirements needs the full clause in this exercise.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
import json

policy = "Refund requests are allowed through calendar day 30 after delivery with a valid receipt."
help_text = "Support can answer questions in Orders."
reference_answer = "You may request a refund within the published window."
rubric_version = "refund-guidance-v1"
history = "The customer said the item is an eligible unused online purchase."
question = "How long after delivery can I request a refund for this order, and what do I need?"
question_b = "Can I still request a refund for my unused online order 45 days after delivery?"
routes = {1: "REJECT", 2: "REVIEW", 3: "REVIEW", 4: "ACCEPT", 5: "ACCEPT", None: "REVIEW"}
fields = {"score", "decision", "reason", "evidence"}
deadline_span = "through calendar day 30 after delivery"
receipt_span = "with a valid receipt"
approved_spans = {policy, deadline_span, receipt_span}

cases = [
    ("A", "Request a refund with a valid receipt through calendar day 30 after delivery, including day 30.", True, "inclusive", "required", False),
    ("B", "You can request a refund within 90 calendar days after delivery with a valid receipt.", True, "wrong", "required", False),
    ("C", "You may request a refund within 30 calendar days after delivery.", True, "correct", "missing", False),
    ("D", "The valid receipt is needed, and delivery is the starting point for the 30-calendar-day refund request window.", True, "correct", "required", False),
    ("E", "Every eligible request is automatically approved within 30 calendar days of delivery if you show a valid receipt.", True, "correct", "required", True),
    ("F", "You can request a refund within 30 calendar days after delivery with a valid receipt.", False, "unclear", "unclear", False),
    ("G", "With a valid receipt you may request a refund up to and including calendar day 30 after delivery.", True, "inclusive", "required", False),
    ("H", "Ask for a refund within 30 calendar days of delivery and have your valid receipt.", True, "correct", "required", False),
]

saved = {
    "A": '{"score":5,"decision":"ACCEPT","reason":"Both requirements and inclusion of day 30 are explicit.","evidence":"Refund requests are allowed through calendar day 30 after delivery with a valid receipt."}',
    "B": '{"score":4,"decision":"ACCEPT","reason":"The reply states the correct window and mentions a receipt.","evidence":"through calendar day 30 after delivery"}',
    "C": '{"score":3,"decision":"REVIEW","reason":"The receipt requirement is absent.","evidence":"with a valid receipt"}',
    "D": '{"score":4,"decision":"ACCEPT","reason":"Both requirements are present.","evidence":"Refund requests are allowed through calendar day 30 after delivery with a valid receipt."}',
    "E": '{"score":4,"decision":"ACCEPT","reason":"The store promises automatic approval.","evidence":"All eligible requests are automatically approved."}',
    "F": '{"score":null,"decision":"REVIEW","reason":"The approved policy is missing.","evidence":""}',
    "G": '{"score":5',
    "H": '{"score":6,"decision":"ACCEPT","reason":"Both requirements are present.","evidence":"with a valid receipt"}',
}
human = {
    "A": 5, "B": 1, "C": 3, "D": 4, "E": 2, "F": None, "G": 5, "H": 4,
}

def validate(raw):
    try:
        verdict = json.loads(raw)
    except json.JSONDecodeError:
        return None, "JSON parse error"
    if not isinstance(verdict, dict) or set(verdict) != fields:
        return None, "schema fields"
    score = verdict["score"]
    if score is not None and (type(score) is not int or score not in routes):
        return None, "score outside schema"
    if verdict["decision"] != routes[score]:
        return None, "decision outside schema"
    if not isinstance(verdict["reason"], str) or not verdict["reason"].strip():
        return None, "reason outside schema"
    evidence = verdict["evidence"]
    if not isinstance(evidence, str) or (score is None and evidence != ""):
        return None, "evidence outside schema"
    if score is not None and not evidence.strip():
        return None, "evidence outside schema"
    return verdict, None

def anchor(source_available, deadline, receipt, promise):
    if not source_available:
        return None
    if deadline == "wrong" or receipt == "waived":
        return 1
    if "unclear" in (deadline, receipt):
        return None
    if promise:
        return 2
    facts = int(deadline in ("inclusive", "correct")) + int(receipt == "required")
    if facts == 0:
        return 2
    if facts == 1:
        return 3
    return 5 if deadline == "inclusive" else 4

def inspect(verdict, problem, source, expected, deadline, receipt):
    if problem:
        return "UNSCORABLE", problem
    score = verdict["score"]
    if expected is None and score is None:
        return "ABSTAINED", "source missing" if source is None else "facts ambiguous"
    if source is None:
        return "UNSCORABLE", "source missing"
    if score is None:
        return "UNSCORABLE", "unexpected abstention"
    if verdict["evidence"] not in approved_spans or verdict["evidence"] not in source:
        return "UNSCORABLE", "unsupported citation"
    if score != expected:
        return "UNSCORABLE", "rubric mismatch"
    relevant_span = policy
    if score == 1 and deadline == "wrong":
        relevant_span = deadline_span
    elif score == 1 and receipt == "waived":
        relevant_span = receipt_span
    elif score == 3:
        relevant_span = receipt_span if receipt == "missing" else deadline_span
    if verdict["evidence"] not in (policy, relevant_span):
        return "UNSCORABLE", "citation misses requirement"
    return "USABLE", "quote and anchor checked"

audit = []
for name, candidate, has_source, deadline, receipt, promise in cases:
    source = policy + "\n" + help_text if has_source else None
    expected = anchor(has_source, deadline, receipt, promise)
    verdict, problem = validate(saved[name])
    status, why = inspect(verdict, problem, source, expected, deadline, receipt)
    if expected == 1:
        decision = "REJECT"
    elif status == "USABLE":
        decision = routes[verdict["score"]]
    else:
        decision = "REVIEW"
    record = {
        "id": name, "question": question_b if name == "B" else question, "history": history,
        "candidate": candidate, "reference_example": reference_answer, "source": source,
        "policy_version": "refund-1" if has_source else None,
        "rubric_version": rubric_version, "raw_judge": saved[name], "judge_result": verdict,
        "reviewed_facts": (deadline, receipt, promise), "status": status, "reason": why,
        "candidate_decision": decision, "human_score": human[name],
    }
    audit.append(record)
    print(f"{name}: {status} ({why}) | candidate {decision} | human {human[name]}")

scored = [r for r in audit if r["status"] == "USABLE"]
checked = [r for r in scored if r["human_score"] is not None]
matches = sum(r["judge_result"]["score"] == r["human_score"] for r in checked)
```

## Knowledge Check Answers

1. Yes. Each call rates one candidate against its own inputs and the rubric. Comparing the resulting scores later is separate from asking a model to pick between two candidates.
2. Supply the approved clause and version for this order, including that the 30 calendar days count from delivery. The example answer alone leaves that fact unstated.
3. The rubric is about refund guidance. Its first anchor gives priority to an explicit contradiction. Score readability in a separate review.
4. Name the criterion and rewrite “good” as observable requirements. Give an example showing the difference between a correct deadline alone and an answer that also includes the receipt.
5. The schema check rejects it. JSON quotes turn the digit into text, while the numeric anchor requires an integer.
6. The quote establishes the receipt requirement. Read the candidate for the receipt condition, and assign the omission anchor if the deadline is correct and no more serious issue is present.
7. Line up the response ID, text, policy version, and rubric. Check the conflicting claim against the approved clause. Agreement between the replays is evidence about consistency only.
8. Save the raw output alongside the case ID, question, candidate, rubric and source versions, and parsing error. Mark the verdict unscorable and route the candidate to review. Attach the reviewer’s outcome when available.

## Common Mistakes

Keep the original candidate and policy version. If two reviewers rate different drafts, their score difference cannot isolate a judging problem.

Check the reason and its evidence as well as the rating. A genuine but irrelevant quote, a legitimate omission, and an explicit contradiction call for different actions.

Report abstentions and unusable verdicts beside agreement. Otherwise a report could look strong because it quietly counted only the cases that were easy to validate.

## Lesson Summary and Next Step

Pointwise judging applies a rubric to one saved response at a time. Keep each criterion clear, define what every rating looks like, and verify the verdict in layers. When the source or judgment is unusable, preserve the evidence and route the case for review. A verified policy contradiction can block the answer independently of the judge’s output.
