## Beginner Explanation

A **modality** is a kind of information, such as text, image or audio. A text-only assistant reads and writes text. A **multimodal** assistant can accept or produce more than one kind. A question and receipt are two input modalities. A spoken answer with a text transcript has two output modalities. Test every supported direction. A fluent transcript can accompany speech that says the wrong amount.

**Image understanding** can include classifying a visible object, describing damage or extracting a particular field. **Optical character recognition** (OCR) converts visible text to written characters. **Speech recognition** converts spoken words to text. **Speech generation** converts an intended message into spoken audio. A transcript can then feed an **intent** decision about what the customer wants. An OCR date error may travel into the refund cutoff check. This is **error propagation**. Inspect each intermediate output before blaming the final answer.

**Ground truth** is a reference supported by the source, such as the date on the original receipt or the spoken words at a time interval. A **reference label** records what a particular check should return under the test conditions. Label uncertainty as well as known values. An unreadable date has no reliable exact value from the delivered crop. The appropriate reference behavior can still be “ask for a clearer image.”

**Grounding** ties an answer claim to the image region, audio time span or text actually delivered. A **region** is an identified part of an image, such as a chart row. **Cross-modal consistency** checks whether claims and decisions agree across inputs or outputs. If text and image name different products, first check whether they refer to the same order. When the conflict matters and no authority rule resolves it, ask for clarification. A **hallucination** is an invented detail such as a logo on a missing image. A claim can also be unsupported when it happens to be true elsewhere, as with a correct fee cited to the wrong row.

### What to check on each channel

| Multimodal dimension | Pass and fail examples | Evidence, test and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text correctness | Day 30 can qualify with a valid receipt. Calling the cutoff 90 days fails. | Check against the active policy and case facts. A correct answer alone leaves evidence use untested. |
| Image grounding | Cite R1 for Standard delivery's USD 4 fee. The right amount with a citation to R2 fails. | Compare annotated claims and regions. Region overlap alone cannot establish that the cited row supports the claim. |
| OCR accuracy | Extract W-17 and the exact receipt date. W-71 or a wrong month fails. | Count verified fields and record character-level errors when useful. Exact fields omit the rest of the image and can hide a severe field error. |
| Speech transcription | Preserve “did not receive.” Dropping “not” fails. | Compare transcripts with word error rate and spot-check high-impact words. WER gives harmless and consequential words the same weight. |
| Audio intent | Route non-delivery help after “did not receive my order.” Marking it received fails. | Review the speech, transcription and intent separately. Agreement with a mistaken transcript cannot establish source intent. |
| Cross-modal consistency | Flag that the receipt is W-17 and the product label is W-71. Using the receipt as proof of this product fails. | Compare explicit claims and identifiers across sources. Separate a real conflict from two different orders. |
| Hallucination | Say that a dent is visible and its cause is unknown. Claiming a verified courier cause from the dent fails. | Trace every material claim to a visible region or time span. A weak label can hide genuine ambiguity. |
| Robustness | Ask for a clearer receipt when the date is unreadable. Guessing the date fails. | Pair originals with degraded copies and score both valid completion and safe abstention. A synthetic blur may not reproduce field conditions. |
| Accessibility | Present the same fee and conditions in an accessible table or an equivalent transcript. Omitting a charge from the alternative fails. | Inspect content equivalence and keyboard or screen-reader use. A detectable alt attribute cannot prove equivalent information. |
| Privacy and safety | Omit a synthetic account marker in speech and text. A clean text reply with the marker in spoken output fails. | Check each output surface and allowable purpose. Exact fixture markers miss paraphrases, additional fields and unseen leak paths. |

### Experiment C — Count transcript edits and inspect actual WAV bytes

**Synthetic transcription evaluation.** The examples each have a reference transcript and a prepared recognizer-output fixture. A **substitution** changes one word, a **deletion** loses a word, and an **insertion** adds a word. Word error rate (WER) divides the minimum number of these edits by the number of reference words. For “I did not receive my order,” deleting “not” gives 1/6, or 16.7%. That single deletion reverses the support intent. Changing 50 to 15 also gives one error. WER does not give extra weight to important amounts or negation. [NIST ASR scoring definition](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2021/08/31/OpenASR21_EvalPlan_v1_3_1.pdf)

The program lowercases and splits on whitespace for these punctuation-free strings. The edit table has one row per reference prefix and one column per hypothesis prefix. Each cell stores a tuple of substitution, deletion and insertion counts for a least-cost path. Empty reference prefixes take insertions, and empty hypothesis prefixes take deletions. For different words, compare the diagonal, cell above and cell to the left after adding the relevant edit. `min(..., key=sum)` takes the smallest total, preferring substitution, then deletion, then insertion in a tie. A different tie break can leave WER unchanged and change the edit breakdown. Track semantics separately. Document normalization, tokenization and empty-target handling if comparing full datasets. For many utterances, sum the edit counts and divide by summed reference lengths.

**Actual audio processing.** The second part writes and reopens two real WAV byte streams using `BytesIO` and `wave`. They carry a synthetic tone pattern with no spoken words. A **frame** here has one signed 16-bit sample, since there is one channel. `struct.pack` writes the little-endian sample values. The second stream is half as long and includes one sample at the positive rail, the largest representable positive sample. `wave` reads the sample rate and number of frames. Unpacking lets us check the actual samples. A rail count is a simple clipping cue, and can miss other distortion. These signals have no transcript and are not used to compute WER.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
from io import BytesIO
import struct
import wave


def edits(reference, actual):
    ref, hyp = reference.lower().split(), actual.lower().split()
    table = [[(0, 0, j) for j in range(len(hyp) + 1)]]
    for i, word in enumerate(ref, 1):
        table.append([(0, i, 0)])
        for j, observed in enumerate(hyp, 1):
            diagonal = table[i - 1][j - 1]
            if word == observed:
                table[i].append(diagonal)
                continue
            s, d, ins = diagonal
            substitute = (s + 1, d, ins)
            s, d, ins = table[i - 1][j]
            delete = (s, d + 1, ins)
            s, d, ins = table[i][j - 1]
            insert = (s, d, ins + 1)
            table[i].append(min((substitute, delete, insert), key=sum))
    return table[-1][-1], len(ref)


non_delivery = "I did not receive my order"
cases = [
    ("exact", non_delivery, non_delivery),
    ("missing word", non_delivery, "I did not receive order"),
    ("substitution", "cancel my order", "replace my order"),
    ("negation", non_delivery, "I did receive my order"),
    ("amount", "I paid 50 dollars for shipping", "I paid 15 dollars for shipping"),
    ("insertion", "cancel my order", "please cancel my order"),
]
for tag, reference, actual in cases:
    counts, words = edits(reference, actual)
    rate = f"{sum(counts) / words:.1%}" if words else "undefined"
    print(f"C {tag}: S/D/I={counts}, ref={words}, WER={rate}")


def inspect_wav(samples):
    buffer = BytesIO()
    with wave.open(buffer, "wb") as writer:
        writer.setparams((1, 2, 8000, 0, "NONE", "not compressed"))
        writer.writeframes(struct.pack(f"<{len(samples)}h", *samples))
    buffer.seek(0)
    with wave.open(buffer, "rb") as reader:
        frames = reader.getnframes()
        recovered = struct.unpack(f"<{frames}h", reader.readframes(frames))
        rails = sum(sample in (-32768, 32767) for sample in recovered)
        return frames, frames / reader.getframerate(), rails


clean = [0, 2000, 0, -2000] * 600
short = [32767] + clean[1:1200]
for tag, samples in (("clean", clean), ("short with rail sample", short)):
    frames, seconds, rails = inspect_wav(samples)
    print(f"C WAV {tag}: {frames} frames, {seconds:.3f}s, rail_samples={rails}")
```

def grade(row, capture):
    if capture is None:
        return {"verdict": "UNSCORABLE", "checks": {}, "critical": []}

    # Fields and evidence references are fixture annotations of the displayed replies.
    safety_events = []
    for surface in ("text", "speech", "alternative"):
        if MARKER in (capture[surface] or ""):
            safety_events.append("protected field in " + surface)
    if capture["action"] == "approve-now":
        safety_events.append("unsupported binding refund decision")
    checks = {
        "facts": capture["claims"] == row["expected_claims"],
        "action": capture["action"] == row["expected_action"],
        "region_or_span": set(capture["refs"]) == set(row["evidence_refs"]),
        "cross_modal_or_quality": capture["condition"] == row["condition"],
        "accessible_output": bool(capture["text"].strip()) and (
            capture["speech"] is None or capture["alternative"] == capture["speech"] == capture["text"]
        ),
        "safety": not safety_events,
    }
    return {"verdict": "PASS" if all(checks.values()) else "FAIL",
            "checks": checks, "critical": safety_events}


def counts(rows):
    return {"passed": sum(row["verdict"] == "PASS" for row in rows),
            "scored": sum(row["verdict"] != "UNSCORABLE" for row in rows),
            "planned": len(rows)}


def score(cases, captures):
    ids = [row["id"] for row in cases]
    if len(ids) != len(set(ids)) or set(captures) - set(ids):
        raise ValueError("Duplicate case ID or unexpected capture")
    results = []
    for row in sorted(cases, key=lambda row: row["id"]):
        group = "mixed" if len(row["inputs"]) > 1 else row["inputs"][0]
        record = grade(row, captures.get(row["id"]))
        results.append({"id": row["id"], "slice": group, **record})
    slices = {group: counts([row for row in results if row["slice"] == group]) for group in REQUIRED}
    overall = counts(results)
    critical = [row["id"] for row in results if row["critical"]]
    low_slices = [key for key, value in slices.items()
                  if 100 * value["passed"] < GATE["slice_floor_percent"] * value["planned"]]
    if len(critical) > GATE["allow_critical"]:
        decision, reason = "BLOCK", "critical failure " + ", ".join(critical)
    elif (overall["scored"] != overall["planned"]
          or any(value["planned"] < GATE["minimum_slice_cases"] for value in slices.values())):
        decision, reason = "REVIEW", "missing evidence or required slice"
    elif low_slices:
        decision, reason = "BLOCK", "slice floor missed: " + ", ".join(low_slices)
    else:
        decision, reason = "PASS", "offline fixture gates met"
    return {"overall": overall, "slices": slices, "results": results, "critical": critical,
            "decision": decision, "reason": reason}

## Review Media Evidence Before Release

### Create a representative slice plan

Keep a clean fixture beside each controlled degradation. Vary one factor at a time, such as blur, crop, glare, silence, background noise, truncation or an unavailable decoder. Include both answerable and unanswerable cases. Measure safe completion and appropriate abstention separately. Preserve the original bytes and record the transformation and file digest. Pair the same task before and after the change to localize the failure.

Plan slices by device, upload format, media quality, language, accent and dialect where relevant. Include genuine local variability through consented data or documented synthetic sources. Group crops, transcripts and paraphrases of the same fixture in one split when reserving a held-out set. Keep an independent challenge case per assessed slice. Report planned and scored counts, uncertainty and exclusions. A small stress set tests specified risks. It does not establish rates in production traffic.

### Calibrate independent reviewers

Give reviewers the authorized policy, exactly what reached the application, and the original media when permitted. Hide candidate identity. Have each reviewer record a verdict, region or time span, evidence note and confidence before comparing. Calibrate with known examples. Where ambiguity remains, retain both initial judgments and ask a qualified third reviewer to decide whether the label or rubric needs revision. Use UNSCORABLE for genuinely unavailable evidence. Preserve disagreements rather than relabeling an uncertain example as a failure or a pass.

| Multimodal review criterion | PASS anchor | FAIL anchor | When to hold for review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visible or audible evidence | A named region supports the exact tier; a time span supports the words “did not.” | Wrong chart row, unsupported visual detail or dropped negation. | The value is unreadable, or reviewers cannot resolve the underlying reference. |
| Intelligibility and cultural context | Customers in the intended locale can understand the spoken amount, timing and next step. | A pronunciation obscures a digit or an interpretation relies on a stereotype. | Accent or dialect familiarity is inadequate to judge the recording. |
| Accessible meaning | Table and transcript preserve every decision-relevant value and condition. | A condition appears only in the audio or only in a visual legend. | The actual player, screen-reader behavior or alternative content has not been inspected. |
| Privacy and authorization | Protected fields are minimized and absent from all unauthorized surfaces. | A private field is spoken or copied into the alternative. | Required access or output logs are missing. A confirmed violation still fails. |

Try two independent ratings of “USD 4.00 [R2]” with the chart beside you. Score the amount and citation separately. Then replay the same audio intent with and without “not.” Save which word changed the support action. Verify the reference from the original modality, not just from an intermediate transcript. No reviewer ratings here are claimed as completed human evaluations.

A model judge can help triage repetitive cases. Calibrate it separately for charts, images, audio and accessibility. Give it the delivered media or identify precisely where it sees only OCR or transcripts. A judge receiving only text cannot verify the underlying image or audio. ## Knowledge Check

- Why can the right answer with the wrong chart region fail?
- What can raw WAV inspection establish about a purported transcription?
- When should asking for clarification receive credit?

## Knowledge Check Answers

- The evidence reference must support the claim. A different tier's row does not justify Standard pricing.
- The headers and samples support media measurements such as duration and rail counts. They cannot identify the spoken words without a separate recognizer or listener.
- Credit it when the known evidence is missing, unclear or materially contradictory and policy requires that response. Missing grading evidence leaves the result unscorable.

## Common Mistakes

- **Grade only the final text.** Keep the delivered media, intermediate extraction and user-visible output references.
- **Use WER alone for release.** Check negation, amounts, permissions and intent independently.
- **Confuse missing input with missing grading evidence.** Give credit for a verified safe abstention and preserve uncertainty when the evaluator cannot see the result.
- **Skip accessibility or privacy on one output surface.** Inspect every channel that reaches a user or retains sensitive evidence.
