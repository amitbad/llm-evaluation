## Why This Lesson Matters

Your Harbor Leaf Market parcel arrived 45 days ago. One draft says you still have time to request a refund because it allows 90 days. The other gives the store’s real 30-day deadline and asks for a receipt. If you’re reviewing the drafts side by side, the policy gives you a clear reason to choose. The harder question comes when two drafts get the facts right and the reviewer still has to decide whether either one is better.

## Beginner Explanation

### Compare the same answers in two ways

| Stable ID | Frozen response |
| --- | --- |
| A | Yes, you may request a refund through calendar day 30 after delivery with a valid receipt. |
| B | Absolutely. You have 90 calendar days after delivery to request a refund, and a receipt is optional. |
| C | Bring your valid receipt and ask for your money back up to and including calendar day 30 after your order arrives. |
| D | Please contact customer support about refunds. |

Give A to a reviewer on its own. Ask whether it states the deadline, the starting event, and the receipt rule correctly. This is **pointwise evaluation**. It answers an absolute question such as whether a reply passes a policy check.

Now put A beside B and ask which reply gives the customer better refund guidance. This is **pairwise evaluation**. It can be easier to see why one draft is better than to decide whether a draft deserves 3 or 4 on a scale. The two approaches answer different questions. A reply can win a recorded preference and still fail the independent policy check.

### Write the rule before showing the pair

Give both candidates the same saved question, approved policy, and rubric. Give the judge the same instructions in every position under rubric `pairwise-v1` and ask it to return `A`, `B`, `TIE`, or `REVIEW`, plus a brief reason quoting the relevant reply and policy words. In this output format `A` and `B` are display slots. Keep the stable candidate IDs in the audit record, where they remain the same between displays.

- **Correctness first.** A reply that follows the policy wins over one with a confirmed critical contradiction. Check the exact number, calendar-day unit, delivery event, and receipt requirement.
- **Completeness next.** When the two replies preserve the facts, prefer the one that answers more of the question. A generic referral can avoid giving wrong advice while leaving the requested guidance missing.
- **Clarity and conciseness last.** Compare how easy the replies are to follow and whether extra words help the customer. A confident tone or polished formatting cannot rescue a wrong rule.
- **TIE** means you compared both answers with sufficient evidence and found no meaningful difference under the rubric. For A and C, both give day 30 and require a valid receipt. A reviewer can tie them if both are equally usable.
- **REVIEW** means a winner or tie cannot be justified yet. If a reply says “first month” and no approved source gives that phrase a clear meaning, leave the comparison unresolved. A tie would imply the evidence had already supported equivalence.

### Swap the displays and follow the stable IDs

Hide system names and use a recorded random assignment to choose the first display order across cases. This is **blind randomization**. Then show each selected pair in the opposite order in a separate judging pass. With two displays per pair, each candidate appears once in each slot. Preserve both records. Keep the question, policy, rubric, judge, and answer text fixed so you can inspect what changed.

Write the stable ID under each slot before you read a saved verdict. In the first display, slot A contains candidate A. After the swap, slot B contains that same answer. A judge that picks slot A first and slot B after the swap has followed the answer. A judge that picks slot A both times has followed the position in this replay, and its stable winner changes. Flag a tie that becomes a win as well. Keep REVIEW and malformed results visible. Under this lesson’s rule, two matching displays support one pair on one question. [Zheng et al., Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/91f18a1287b398d378ef22505bf41832-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html)

For other presentation checks, hold the policy facts fixed. Put the same terms in a list or a paragraph to test formatting. Add “Certainly” without changing the facts to test confidence. Vary length separately from extra useful information. A result on one imagined pair tells you which comparison to inspect next. Repeat across several matched cases and saves before describing a preference as systematic.

Two judges can each be consistent under the swap and still prefer different answers. Keep their normalized outcomes separately. That is **multi-model uncertainty** when the judges are different models. Compare their cited evidence and ask a human reviewer to resolve important disagreements. Several judges can miss the same policy fact, so a majority still needs a factual check.

### Count supported pairs and show the coverage

Start with one point for a win, half a point per candidate for a tie, and zero for a loss. This chapter uses these points as a simple summary. Take two wins and a tie. The points are 1 + 1 + 0.5 = 2.5, the win rate is 2/3, and the points per scored pair are 2.5/3. The two fractions answer different questions because the second gives partial credit for ties.

With four candidates, there are six possible unordered pairs. If you can score four of them, coverage is 4/6. Say which pairs were absent and which were attempted but unresolved. A candidate with two wins from three pairs can have more raw points than one with one win from one pair. Report both the pair counts and the points per pair. Normalizing helps expose unequal exposure. It cannot correct for different opponents, missing hard cases, or a player with no scored pairs. Report an unavailable rate for a zero denominator. Avoid treating a tied points total as a head-to-head tie.

### Separate a summary from a defensible ranking

Draw an arrow from each winner to the loser. If A beats B, B beats C, and C beats A, the arrows form a **cycle**. A wins against B and loses to C, so no ordering puts every observed winner ahead of every loser. This pattern is called **non-transitivity**. Keep the cycle visible even if a points table has a top row. With incomplete comparisons, explain which extra pair would help before releasing a ranking.

A model such as **Bradley–Terry** tries to explain repeated decisive comparisons using one positive strength per candidate. If two fitted strengths were 2 and 1, the model would give the first candidate a win probability of 2 ÷ (2 + 1), about 0.67, for that matchup. The strengths come from a fit to many comparisons and need not equal raw points. The basic model handles wins and losses. Ties and abstentions need an explicitly chosen policy or extension. A single strength cannot reproduce all directions in a cycle, and disconnected groups lack evidence for a common ranking. Paired replays also share a question and need appropriate treatment as dependent observations. This chapter teaches the intuition and uses transparent points for its own report. [Bradley and Terry, Rank Analysis of Incomplete Block Designs](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/39.3-4.324)

Keep the raw comparison ID, order, judge identity, question, frozen candidates, policy, rubric version, outcome, evidence, and reverse result. Record which results entered the aggregation, the ranking method, and all open review flags. A relative preference can put B above D even though B fails the critical refund gate. Keep that gate alongside the preference report before deciding which answers a customer can see.

## Worked Demonstration

### Example 1. A plausible draft loses on the policy

**User question.** Can I get a refund for something I bought?

**Approved reference.** Harbor Leaf allows eligible requests through calendar day 30 after delivery. A valid receipt is required.

**Actual LLM response.** A says “Yes, you may request a refund through calendar day 30 after delivery with a valid receipt.” B says “Absolutely. You have 90 calendar days after delivery to request a refund, and a receipt is optional.”

**Assessment.** Check the starting event, date, and receipt first. A preserves all three. In B, “90 calendar days” contradicts the 30-day limit and “a receipt is optional” contradicts the requirement. A wins. The word “Absolutely” cannot change either fact. If you reviewed B pointwise, it would independently fail the refund-policy check.

**Failure classification.** Save A as the winner and record B’s two contradictions. The comparison supports a preference for this question. It gives no ranking of the systems on other refund questions.

**User question.** Can I get a refund for something I bought?

**Approved reference.** Use `refund-1`, and prefer the correct deadline and receipt condition before style.

### Step 6 — Find a cycle and show missing pairs

Represent a win as a tuple `(winner, loser)`. `combinations(ids, 2)` gives each unordered pair once. `tuple(sorted(edge))` puts both directions of a pair under the same key for coverage. For a three-way cycle, test the two directions starting at the earliest ID in each group of three. This small check detects three-candidate cycles.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
from itertools import combinations

ids = ("A", "B", "C", "D")
edges = {("A", "B"), ("B", "C"), ("C", "A")}
all_pairs = set(combinations(ids, 2))
scored = {tuple(sorted(edge)) for edge in edges}
cycles = []
for a, b, c in combinations(ids, 3):
    for x, y in ((b, c), (c, b)):
        if {(a, x), (x, y), (y, a)} <= edges:
            cycles.append(f"{a}>{x}>{y}>{a}")

missing = [a + b for a, b in sorted(all_pairs - scored)]
print(f"Scored pairs: {len(scored)}/{len(all_pairs)}")
print(f"Missing: {', '.join(missing)}")
print(f"Three-way cycles: {', '.join(cycles) or 'none'}")
```

### Step 7 — Build an auditable pairwise report

Replay all six unordered pairs. The first and reversed rows keep their display order, raw choice, and saved reason. These are stored examples of a balanced schedule, with one display in each order per pair. J1 is the primary fictional model judge. J2 is a different fictional model that supplies only a comparison on B/D. A reversal that changes the stable winner, or an abstention in either display, sends the pair to review. The baseline points summarize supported J1 results. It flags a stable J2 disagreement separately. A sensitivity check can withhold that pair from the table.

The program checks the reviewed policy facts separately. A confirmed wrong claim is FAIL, and missing facts need REVIEW. These values are supplied by a policy reader. The program neither interprets free text nor runs a judge. Its cycle check uses only decisive results included in the points table. It tries each possible path through a group of candidates once per starting ID. `permutations()` provides the possible orders after the first ID. `path[1:] + path[:1]` shifts the path by one step and wraps back to its start, so `zip()` pairs every stop with the next.

The dictionary `audit` preserves each pair and its two displays. The list `rankable` contains only candidates with a scored appearance. A missing reverse stays open. `[::-1]` reverses a tuple so the two orders can be checked before counting anything. A sorted set of their scores makes the report deterministic. Group equal points together and keep a total ranking provisional if coverage, cycles, or judge disagreements leave open questions.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
from itertools import combinations, permutations

question = "Can I get a refund for something I bought?"
policy = "Refund requests are allowed through calendar day 30 after delivery with a valid receipt."
policy_version = "refund-1"
rubric = "pairwise-v1"
aggregation = "primary"
# Frozen text, verified day count, and whether a valid receipt is required.
candidates = {
    "A": ("Yes, you may request a refund through calendar day 30 after delivery with a valid receipt.", 30, True),
    "B": ("Absolutely. You have 90 calendar days after delivery to request a refund, and a receipt is optional.", 90, False),
    "C": ("Bring your valid receipt and ask for your money back up to and including calendar day 30 after your order arrives.", 30, True),
    "D": ("Please contact customer support about refunds.", None, None),
}
# Each row keeps order, raw slot choice, and the saved judge's reason.
primary = {
    "AB": [(("A", "B"), "A", "chosen answer preserves the 30-day limit and receipt"),
           (("B", "A"), "B", "chosen answer preserves the 30-day limit and receipt")],
    "AC": [(("A", "C"), "TIE", "both preserve the required facts"),
           (("C", "A"), "TIE", "both preserve the required facts")],
    "AD": [(("A", "D"), "A", "chosen answer provides the deadline and receipt"),
           (("D", "A"), "B", "chosen answer provides the deadline and receipt")],
    "BC": [(("B", "C"), "B", "C preserves the 30-day limit"),
           (("C", "B"), "B", "B sounds decisive")],
    "BD": [(("B", "D"), "A", "B gives more detail"),
           (("D", "B"), "B", "B gives more detail")],
    "CD": [(("C", "D"), "REVIEW", "no supported comparison supplied"),
           (("D", "C"), "REVIEW", "no supported comparison supplied")],
}
secondary = {
    "BD": [(("B", "D"), "B", "D avoids giving the wrong deadline"),
           (("D", "B"), "A", "D avoids giving the wrong deadline")],
}

def normalize(order, choice):
    if choice in ("A", "B"):
        return order[0 if choice == "A" else 1]
    return choice if choice in ("TIE", "REVIEW") else "INVALID"

def supported(pair, views):
    if len(views) != 2:
        return "REVIEW", "missing reverse"
    first, reverse = views
    if set(first[0]) != set(pair) or reverse[0] != first[0][::-1]:
        return "REVIEW", "invalid display orders"
    outcomes = [normalize(order, choice) for order, choice, reason in views]
    if any(result in ("REVIEW", "INVALID") for result in outcomes):
        return "REVIEW", "abstention or invalid"
    if outcomes[0] != outcomes[1]:
        return "REVIEW", "order conflict"
    return outcomes[0], "supported"

def gate(days, receipt):
    if (days is not None and days != 30) or receipt is False:
        return "FAIL"
    if days is None or receipt is None:
        return "REVIEW"
    return "PASS"

ids = sorted(candidates)
counts = {name: dict(wins=0, losses=0, ties=0) for name in ids}
audit, edges, open_pairs, judge_disputes = {}, set(), [], []
scored = 0
print(f"{policy_version} | {rubric} | aggregation {aggregation}")
for a, b in combinations(ids, 2):
    pair = a + b
    views = primary.get(pair, [])
    result, reason = supported(pair, views)
    other_views = secondary.get(pair, [])
    other, _ = supported(pair, other_views)
    dispute = result != "REVIEW" and other != "REVIEW" and result != other
    if dispute:
        judge_disputes.append(pair)
    if dispute and aggregation == "exclude-disputes":
        result, reason = "REVIEW", "judge disagreement"
    audit[pair] = {
        "id": "P-" + pair, "question": question, "source": policy, "policy_version": policy_version,
        "rubric": rubric, "aggregation": aggregation, "stable_ids": (a, b),
        "candidate_texts": {name: candidates[name][0] for name in (a, b)},
        "J1_displays": views, "J2_displays": other_views, "judge_dispute": dispute,
        "table_result": result, "reason": reason,
    }
    choices = "/".join(normalize(order, choice) for order, choice, _ in views) or "none"
    print(f"P-{pair}: J1 {choices} | table {result} ({reason})")
    if result == "REVIEW":
        open_pairs.append(pair)
        continue
    scored += 1
    if result == "TIE":
        counts[a]["ties"] += 1
        counts[b]["ties"] += 1
    else:
        loser = b if result == a else a
        counts[result]["wins"] += 1
        counts[loser]["losses"] += 1
        edges.add((result, loser))

points, rankable, cycles = {}, [], []
for name, row in counts.items():
    wins, losses, ties = row["wins"], row["losses"], row["ties"]
    total = wins + losses + ties
    points[name] = wins + 0.5 * ties
    rate = f"{points[name] / total:.3f}" if total else "unavailable"
    if total:
        rankable.append(name)
    print(f"{name}: {wins}W {losses}L {ties}T | {points[name]:.1f} pts | {rate} per pair | policy {gate(*candidates[name][1:])}")

for length in range(3, len(ids) + 1):
    for group in combinations(ids, length):
        start = group[0]
        for tail in permutations(group[1:]):
            path = (start,) + tail
            next_nodes = path[1:] + path[:1]
            if set(zip(path, next_nodes)) <= edges:
                cycles.append(">".join(path + (start,)))
groups = ["=".join(name for name in rankable if points[name] == score)
          for score in sorted({points[name] for name in rankable}, reverse=True)]
attempted = sum(bool(entry["J1_displays"]) for entry in audit.values())
print(f"Attempted coverage: {attempted}/{len(audit)}")
print(f"Scored coverage: {scored}/{len(audit)} | open: {','.join(open_pairs) or 'none'}")
print(f"Cross-judge disputes: {','.join(judge_disputes) or 'none'}")
print(f"Cycles: {','.join(cycles) or 'none'}")
print("Points groups: " + (" > ".join(groups) or "unavailable"))
print("Total ranking: withheld" if open_pairs or judge_disputes or cycles else "Total ranking: points groups")
```

## Common Mistakes

Map each display label back to the stable answer before comparing the outcomes. A raw slot label can point to a different answer after a swap.

Track ties, unresolved attempts, absent pairs, and cross-judge disputes separately. These outcomes change the evidence available to a ranking.

Always check each answer against the approved policy before release. Ranking points summarize relative choices on the collected pairs.
