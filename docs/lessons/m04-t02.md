## What You Will Learn

- Write instructions for reviewers who label a case independently.
- Resolve a disagreement using the full question, the applicable source, and a recorded reason.
- Generate draft cases from a policy without treating proposed labels as reviewed truth.
- Decide which drafts to accept, hold for review, or reject.
- Record where a draft came from and what permission covers its reuse.

## Why This Lesson Matters

Imagine you keep exactly the balance your bank asks for. The assistant says you won’t owe a fee, but the team’s test marks that reply wrong. At Harbor Leaf Bank, one reviewer filled in “fee due” in the answer key and another wrote “no fee.” Before anyone changes the assistant, check what the policy says about a balance right at the minimum.

The question is about a Standard Savings account with exactly ₹5,000 on July 15, 2026. Section 2 of the approved July policy, SV-2#2, says the monthly fee of ₹100 applies **below** ₹5,000. Put the customer’s balance beside that word. Exactly ₹5,000 is at the minimum. A label that says fee due would make a correct “no fee” reply fail the test.

The bank, policy, generated drafts, and reviewer records are fictional. The chapter builds on [Rubrics and a first human evaluation](#m03-t03). If you’re publishing reviewed cases to a versioned dataset, first use [Golden schemas, provenance and versioning](#m04-t01). Allow about six hours for demonstrations, Python experiments, and practice.

## Beginner Explanation

### 1. Give reviewers the same question and the same rule

Imagine you hand someone a printed question and ask them to “mark it right.” They may judge the wording, while you wanted them to check the fee. Instead, tell each reviewer what decision the label represents. Supply the same case ID, account, date, balance if known, and policy excerpt.

Here the label describes what the correct answer should say for the case. It belongs to the case. A later verdict on a particular assistant reply belongs to that captured reply. Adding a label or evidence note to a case is **annotation**. These instructions keep everyone on the same task.

- **FEE_DUE.** Use when the verified July Standard Savings balance is below ₹5,000. Record the approved fee of ₹100 per month.
- **NO_FEE.** Use at ₹5,000 or above. Record that no below-minimum fee applies.
- **NEEDS_REVIEW.** Use when the balance or applicable policy is missing or unclear. Record what would let you decide. Keep the case out of the scored set while it is unresolved.

Ask each reviewer to record a label and the words from the policy that support it before they see the other reviewer's decision. These are **independent labels**. They give you a record of where people could interpret the instructions differently. Independent reviewers can still make the same mistake, so agreement alone gives no guarantee that a label is correct.

### 2. Resolve the disagreement from the evidence

For case S-02 at ₹5,000, reviewer A reads “below” as “at or below” and chooses FEE_DUE. Reviewer B chooses NO_FEE and cites the strict cutoff. Show the full case and approved policy to a designated reviewer who can resolve the disagreement. Have them compare the notes, record a final label and reason, and keep the original labels.

This process is **adjudication**, sometimes called arbitration. The adjudicator should explain the decision by reference to the case and policy. When the source itself is unclear, keep the case unresolved and ask the policy owner. Two votes for one label can’t settle a policy that says something different.

If reviewers keep making the same mistake, change the instructions. Add a worked example at exactly ₹5,000. Keep a record of which instruction version was used and recheck any reviewed labels affected by the change.

### 3. Make more draft cases from one approved source

Start with the policy sentence and pick balances just below, at, and just above the cutoff. Use ₹4,999, ₹5,000 and ₹5,001. Write a customer question for each. Include the account, date, balance, source, and a proposed label in each draft. This is **structured generation** because you’re asking for the same fields every time. You can create drafts with a small program or ask a model for candidates. In this chapter Python supplies the fictional drafts.

Cases created deliberately for practice or testing are **synthetic data**. Three invented balances help test the edge of this rule. They don’t tell you how often actual customers ask those questions. A draft can introduce an invented source, a different account, or a wrong proposed label. A neat table is only the start of review.

Imagine S-01 comes back a month later with a note that says “from the bank’s website.” Another reviewer can’t tell which policy was used or whether the label was checked. Save SV-2#2, the effective date, how the case was made, and who reviewed it. That record of the case’s history is its **provenance**.

Then check what you’re allowed to reuse. A public policy could be readable while its reuse terms remain unclear. Record the permission or **license**, the terms that say what you can copy or share, and keep a reference to those terms alongside the case. Here the bank’s fictional permission covers these excerpts and derived questions for the practice set. If that record is missing, hold the draft while you check with the owner.

### 4. Decide what enters the scored set

A draft that meets the source and review requirements can be accepted. A draft with missing evidence or an unresolved label should be held. A draft that contradicts the approved rule should be rejected.

Keep held cases in a separate review area. This is **quarantine**. They stay available for investigation but don’t count toward a score. Keep the reason for a rejection too. If a reviewer later corrects a defective draft, go through review again before accepting a new version. Preserve the original decisions.

For the lab, accept a draft only if the source applies, the reuse permission is known, the balance is verified within the case, and the proposed label agrees with a reviewed, policy-backed final label. Run simple checks to catch missing fields or an inconsistent proposal. Human review still handles whether the source and language make sense for the question. These examples do not measure real assistant performance.

## Worked Demonstration

### Example 1. Label the same case before discussing it

**User question.** S-01 asks “Will I pay a monthly fee on a Standard Savings balance of ₹4,999 on July 15, 2026?”

**Approved reference.** SV-2#2 applies to Standard Savings on that date and charges ₹100 monthly below ₹5,000.

**Actual LLM response.** The draft generator proposed the label FEE_DUE and the reference reply “A ₹100 monthly fee applies.” This is a fictional draft.

**Assessment.** Reviewer A checks that ₹4,999 lies below ₹5,000 and chooses FEE_DUE. Reviewer B reads the applicable excerpt independently and records the same label and amount. Compare both notes only after they have been saved. The passage supports their shared answer.

**Failure classification.** Accept the row once source and permission checks are complete. The agreement supports the decision on S-01. It gives no estimate of how often reviewers will agree on other wording.

### Example 2. Settle a dispute at exactly the minimum

**User question.** S-02 asks “If my Standard Savings balance is exactly ₹5,000 on July 15, 2026, do I pay the monthly fee?”

**Approved reference.** SV-2#2 charges the fee strictly below ₹5,000. The source and instructions are the same as for S-01.

**Actual LLM response.** The fictional draft answer says “No below-minimum fee applies.” Reviewer A chose FEE_DUE and reviewer B chose NO_FEE.

**Assessment.** Read A’s and B’s labels beside the case. A treated the cutoff as “at or below.” B used “below.” Check the date, then read SV-2#2 and compare the two numbers. ₹5,000 equals ₹5,000, so the balance does not meet the “below” condition. Write the resolution as “S-02, NO_FEE. The stated balance equals the minimum, and SV-2#2 charges only below it.” Keep A’s and B’s labels with that new entry. If the same mistake recurs, add the exact-cutoff example to the guide.

**Failure classification.** The first label used the wrong comparison. Resolve the label without changing the question or the policy. An unresolved policy conflict would need a different decision, to hold the case while the owner clarifies the rule.

### Example 3. A plausible draft still needs review before it counts

**User question.** S-03 asks “Will I pay a fee on my July Standard Savings account?” and omits the balance. S-04 asks the same question with a verified balance of ₹5,001.

**Approved reference.** SV-2#2 is approved and reusable in the fictional practice set. The amount of the balance determines whether the fee applies.

**Actual LLM response.** Both fictional drafts say “A ₹100 monthly fee applies” and propose FEE_DUE. The wording reads like a real answer.

**Assessment.** S-03 gives no balance, so the reviewer can’t verify a fee. Mark NEEDS_REVIEW. S-04 gives an above-threshold balance, and both reviewers choose NO_FEE. Read the source to verify that decision. The draft’s proposed FEE_DUE contradicts the policy for S-04.

**Failure classification.** Hold S-03 outside scoring until the missing information is resolved. Reject S-04 as submitted and review any corrected draft again. Counting both as valid because they cite a real policy would make the set look bigger without making its labels more reliable.

### Step 1 — Turn one policy sentence into an instruction

The approved July policy charges a fee below 5000. Try the instruction with a known balance. The equals sign stores a value in a named variable. The `<` comparison asks whether one number is less than the other. The indented line under `if` runs when the comparison is true. The `else` line selects the other outcome.

Copy the block into the editor and click **Run**.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
balance = 4999
minimum = 5000

if balance < minimum:
    print("Proposed label: FEE_DUE")
else:
    print("Proposed label: NO_FEE")
```

```text

```

The balance is one rupee below the minimum, so the program takes the first branch.

**Experiment.** Change only `balance` to `5000` and run again.

### Step 5 — Generate drafts around a boundary

Start at ₹5,000 and move one rupee in either direction. Subtracting one gives ₹4,999, and adding one gives ₹5,001. Both readings of the rule agree at those balances. Add zero to test the only balance that separates “below” from “at or below.” Each list item holds a name and a number to add, called an offset. `append()` adds the exact-cutoff pair when you ask for it.

The loop builds a dictionary for each draft with the same fields. `str(balance)` converts the balance to text so it can be joined to the ID prefix. The `f` before the question string tells Python to insert the current balance where it sees `{balance}`.

Copy the block into the editor and click **Run**.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
minimum = 5000
source = "SV-2#2"
include_boundary = False
offsets = [("below", -1), ("above", 1)]
if include_boundary:
    offsets.append(("at", 0))

for position, offset in offsets:
    balance = minimum + offset
    if balance < minimum:
        proposal = "FEE_DUE"
    else:
        proposal = "NO_FEE"
    draft = {
        "id": "JULY-" + str(balance),
        "question": f"Does a Standard Savings balance of {balance} rupees incur the monthly fee?",
        "date": "2026-07-15",
        "balance": balance,
        "source": source,
        "proposal": proposal,

    print("DRAFT", draft["id"], position, draft["date"], draft["source"], draft["proposal"])
    print(draft["question"])
```

### Step 7 — Keep accepted, held, and rejected drafts separate

Start with four complete practice records for the July Standard Savings task. The reviewer pairs and the adjudicator’s decision are supplied human judgments. Each case must have the applicable July source, recorded reuse permission, a verified balance, and a supported decision before its draft can be accepted.

A function is a named group of instructions. `def review(case):` takes one draft and `return` hands back its first outcome. `decisions.get(case_id)` gives `None` when there’s no adjudication record. In `final, note = decision`, Python gives the two parts of the decision their own names. `not note` checks for an empty note here. For the known balances, compare the final label to the simple policy rule. A draft label is accepted only if it agrees with that final label.

Copy the block into the editor and click **Run**.

[Open Python Editor](https://www.online-python.com/)

```python
cases = [
    {"id": "S-01", "balance": 4999, "proposal": "FEE_DUE",
     "source": "SV-2#2", "permission": "practice-set reuse"},
    {"id": "S-02", "balance": 5000, "proposal": "NO_FEE",
     "source": "SV-2#2", "permission": "practice-set reuse"},
    {"id": "S-03", "balance": None, "proposal": "FEE_DUE",
     "source": "SV-2#2", "permission": "practice-set reuse"},
    {"id": "S-04", "balance": 5001, "proposal": "FEE_DUE",
     "source": "SV-2#2", "permission": "practice-set reuse"},
]
reviews = {
    "S-01": ("FEE_DUE", "FEE_DUE"),
    "S-02": ("FEE_DUE", "NO_FEE"),
    "S-03": ("NEEDS_REVIEW", "NEEDS_REVIEW"),
    "S-04": ("NO_FEE", "NO_FEE"),
}
decisions = {"S-02": ("NO_FEE", "SV-2#2 charges only below 5000.")}


def review(case):
    if case["source"] != "SV-2#2":
        return "HOLD: confirm applicable source"
    if case["permission"] != "practice-set reuse":
        return "HOLD: confirm reuse permission"
    balance = case["balance"]
    if balance is None:
        return "HOLD: verify balance"

    first, second = reviews[case["id"]]
    if first != second:
        decision = decisions.get(case["id"])
        if decision is None:
            return "HOLD: adjudicate disagreement"
        final, note = decision
        if not note:
            return "HOLD: record adjudication reason"
    else:
        final = first

    if final == "NEEDS_REVIEW":
        return "HOLD: resolve reviewer concern"
    if balance < 5000:
        expected = "FEE_DUE"
    else:
        expected = "NO_FEE"
    if final != expected:
        return "HOLD: recheck decision against policy"
    if case["proposal"] != final:
        return "REJECT: draft label contradicts reviewed label"
    return "ACCEPT: " + final


for case in cases:
    print(case["id"], review(case))
```

```text
S-01 ACCEPT: FEE_DUE
S-02 ACCEPT: NO_FEE
S-03 HOLD: verify balance
S-04 REJECT: draft label contradicts reviewed label
```

S-02 reaches acceptance after its disagreement has a recorded, policy-backed resolution. S-03 stops before scoring because the balance is unknown. S-04 has a supported review decision and a conflicting proposed label.

**Experiment.** Change only the permission value for S-02 from `"practice-set reuse"` to `None`. Run again.

```text

S-02 HOLD: confirm reuse permission
S-03 HOLD: verify balance
S-04 REJECT: draft label contradicts reviewed label
```

S-02 now stops at the permission check. Its original reviewer labels and adjudication stay the same.

**Checkpoint.** Could this checker establish that a real dataset is ready to publish? It checks the supplied rows for this one fictional task. A real team still needs to verify evidence and permissions, review the actual text and policy scope, and preserve the case history before publication.

1. Give both reviewers the same case and source, then collect their labels and evidence notes separately before discussing either answer.
2. Use the approved and applicable SV-2#2. It says below ₹5,000. At the cutoff, their shared label is wrong.
3. Hold it in quarantine with both original judgments and the reason. Ask the policy owner for an approved source and effective date.

1. `S-01 ACCEPT: FEE_DUE`
2. `S-02 ACCEPT: NO_FEE`
3. `S-03 HOLD: verify balance`
4. `S-04 ACCEPT: NO_FEE`

The corrected S-04 answer should say no below-minimum fee applies at ₹5,001. Accept after verifying the source, reuse permission, inputs, and reviewed label. Hold missing evidence or unresolved disagreements apart from the accepted set. Reject a draft that contradicts the reviewed label. The checker uses supplied fields. A human still needs to confirm the underlying source, permission, and question wording. Preserve rejected drafts and review notes when you release a corrected version.

## Common Mistakes

- **Mixing case labels and reply verdicts.** Link verdicts to the exact responses judged and expectations to the cases.
- **Copying a first label.** Record independent decisions before sharing them.
- **Using consensus as proof.** Check the applicable source, especially at the threshold.
- **Counting unresolved drafts as coverage.** Keep held drafts available for review and outside the scored set.
- **Dropping source and permission history.** Save the evidence, permitted use, and review record for each case.

## Lesson Summary and Next Step

Supply the whole case and the same labeling instructions to both reviewers. Keep the independent labels. If they disagree, compare the evidence and record a separate resolution. Generated cases can cover an important edge quickly. Review their source, permission, and label before counting them in the set.
